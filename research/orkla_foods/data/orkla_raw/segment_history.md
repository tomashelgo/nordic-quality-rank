# Orkla food segment: definition history 2000–2026 (with pre-2000 notes)

Prepared 2026-10-06. Latest report used: Q2 2026 (published July 2026).
Companion files in this folder:
- `segment_history.json`: the same content in machine-readable form.
- `restatements.csv`: restated quarterly and annual figures extracted from Orkla's restatement files.
- `geo_weights.csv`: revenue shares by country or region, per segment definition and year.
- `ma_events.csv`: acquisitions and divestments that affected the food segment.

All amounts are NOK million. "OF" = Orkla Foods. Restated figures were checked against the source files. The checks are listed at the end.

## 3. Metric definitions over time

**Revenue.** The segment figure is "operating revenues", which includes intra-group sales; eliminations are shown separately. The geographic notes use external sales revenues.

**Profit measure**

| Period | Segment profit measure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1990s–2004 (NGAAP) | "Operating profit before goodwill amortisation". 1990s annual reports also show operating profit after goodwill. | Called EBITA in the 2002–2004 reports. Before "other revenues and expenses" (restructuring, gains). |
| 2005–2014 (IFRS) | **EBITA (adj.)**: operating profit before amortisation of intangibles and before "other income and expenses" (OIE). | 2011–2012 restated in Apr-2013 for IAS 19R: the pension finance element moved to financial items. This raises EBITA. Use the restated 2011–12 figures for consistency with 2013+. |
| 2015– | **EBIT (adj.)**: operating profit before OIE, i.e. EBITA less amortisation of intangibles. 2014 restated. | Gap to EBITA is tiny for Orkla Foods (~7 m/yr in 2014). OIE covers M&A costs, restructuring and integration costs, major gains and write-downs. |
| 2019– | EBIT (adj.) under **IFRS 16** | No restatement of 2018. Negligible EBIT effect; EBITDA higher. |
| 2023– | Also shows reported EBIT, contribution ratio and ROCE (rolling 12 months) per portfolio company | – |

**Organic growth**

| Period | Term / availability | Definition and treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 2001–2002 | "growth for continuing business" (text) | Adjusted for acquisitions and divestments; FX sometimes mentioned. |
| 2003–2014 | **"underlying" growth** per segment in report text (footnote) | "Excluding acquisitions and divestments and currency translation effects". From ~2013: "…and other considerable structural changes". No tables per segment. |
| 2015Q1– | **"organic growth"** (text per business area) | "Excluding acquired and sold companies and currency translation effects". |
| 2016Q2– | Formal ESMA APM definition plus a table splitting sales growth into organic / FX / structure by business area | Acquired companies are excluded for 12 months after the transaction date. Sold companies are excluded pro forma for the 12 months before the transaction date. FX is neutralised by translating this year's revenue at last year's rates. |
| 2017Q4– | Organic growth row in each business-area table (2016 comparative given) | same |
| 2018Q4– | **Underlying EBIT (adj.) growth** and margin change per business area | Like-for-like EBIT adjusted for M&A and FX on the same rules. |
| 2019 | Quarterly organic growth for Orkla Foods restated in the Feb-2020 file | 1.7 / 3.1 / 0.8 / 1.5%, FY 1.8% |
| 2021– (practice) and 2023Q2– (formal) | Adjustments for material **distribution agreements** won or lost (Panzani, OTA Solgryn 2021; PepsiCo/Tropicana and Alpro 2022–24; Quaker 2026) and for **intra-group transfers** (Frödinge 2020, Oolannin 2022, plant-based production and part of Idun 2022–23, a Danish distribution agreement 2026) | These are treated as "structure", not as organic. |
| 2023Q2– | **Price vs volume/mix split** | Price = net price change to customers after discounts and campaigns, at unchanged volume. Volume/mix = organic growth minus price. |
| Hyperinflation | **No explicit treatment** | Orkla Foods had no operations in hyperinflationary economies. Russia (Hamé) ran to Mar-2022 and was not IAS 29. Organic growth therefore includes local price inflation (e.g. 2022–23 price growth of +10–16% in Orkla Foods Europe). |
| "Underlying" variants | Old "underlying growth" (2003–14) = today's organic growth concept. "Underlying EBIT (adj.)" (2018–) = like-for-like profit. | – |

**Reporting frequency.** Four-monthly until 2000; quarterly from 2001, with 2000 comparatives. Q4 is seasonally the strongest quarter. Easter timing moves sales between Q1 and Q2.

## 5. How to splice a consistent long series

1. **Revenue level.** Chain backwards from F/E using the ratio of revenues in the overlap periods:
   - E→D on 2021H1, 2021Q3, 2021Q4 and 2022Q1–Q4. India is ~10–13% of D.
   - D→C on 2013Q1–2014Q3. Orkla Foods International is ~12–14% of D in 2013–14.
   - C→B on 2011Q1–2012Q2. Confectionery is ~6% of B.
   - B→A: use the 2004–2007 Orkla Foods Nordic quarters (2005–07 definition) inside A. Then switch to the 2008 definition using the 2007 overlap (+257/yr Baltics).
   - Alternatively, chain the **reported organic growth** (y/y, like-for-like) to build an organic revenue index. This avoids most scope breaks.
2. **Margins.** EBITA margins (2004–2014) and EBIT (adj.) margins (2015–) differ by about 0.05pp for Orkla Foods. 2011–2014 should use IAS 19R-restated EBITA. IFRS 16 has no material effect on the EBIT margin.
3. **Known level shifts inside one definition.** These are not restated; use organic growth or dummies:
   - Panda (2005), SladCo (2005), Krupskaya (2006), MTR (2007), Superfish out (2007)
   - Kalev (2010), Bakers out (2012Q1)
   - Rieber (2013Q2), Delecta out (2014Q3)
   - Kavli (2016Q1), Hamé (2016Q2), K-Salat out (2017Q4)
   - Easyfood (2019Q2), Eastern (2021Q2), Russia out (2022Q1)
   - Khell-Food (2023Q1), Põltsamaa out (2026Q2)
4. **Geography weights for macro variables.** Use `geo_weights.csv` with the row matching the segment definition used for each year. Before 2004, use the 2004 proxy.

## 7. Is Orkla Foods data from before 2000 available?

**Quarterly: no, Orkla did not report quarters before 2001.** Orkla published **four-monthly (tertial) interim reports** through 2000. The AR1998 and AR1999 financial calendars list the "first four-monthly report" and "report on first eight months of 2000". The first quarterly report was **Q1 2001**, which also gave quarterly 2000 comparatives. Other agents can therefore build quarters from 2000Q1.

**Tertial (4-month) segment data for 1995–2000 is online** as Orkla reports hosted on Hugin. Examples:

**Structural breaks in the 1990s:**
